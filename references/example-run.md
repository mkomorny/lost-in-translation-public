# Example playthrough

This is what a finished "Lost in Translation" run should feel like — casual,
skimmable, a little theatrical. Use this as a tone/format reference, not a
template to fill in word-for-word.

---

**Original phrase:** "I'll meet you at the coffee shop at 9."

**Chain:** Yoruba → Icelandic → Khmer → Xhosa → English

**Hop 1 — Yoruba:**
"Emi yoo pade re ni ile kofi ni aago mesan."
*(gloss: "I will meet you at the coffee house at the ninth hour")*

**Hop 2 — Icelandic:**
"Ég mun hitta þig í kaffihúsinu klukkan níu."
*(gloss: "I will meet you in the coffee house at nine o'clock" — still holding
together so far)*

**Hop 3 — Khmer:**
"ខ្ញុំនឹងជួបអ្នកនៅហាងកាហ្វេនៅម៉ោងប្រាំបួន"
*(roughly: "I will meet [you] at the coffee shop at hour nine" — Khmer's
sentence structure starts smoothing out some of the original phrasing)*

**Hop 4 — Xhosa:**
"Ndiza kudibana nawe kwivenkile yekofu ngentsimbi yethoba."
*(gloss: "I will link up with you at the coffee store around the ninth
bell" — "meet" has drifted toward "link up," and "9" is now "the ninth
bell")*

**Back to English:**
> "I will link up with you at the coffee store around the ninth bell."

**What changed:** "meet you" became "link up with you," and "at 9" turned
into the vaguely medieval "around the ninth bell." Not bad for four hops —
the core plan (coffee, 9-ish) survived. Want to run it again with a longer
chain and see if it breaks completely?

---

Keep future runs in this shape: show every hop with a light gloss, then the
final result, then one short line noting what actually drifted — not a
line-by-line diff, just the interesting bit. Close with a casual offer to
go again.
